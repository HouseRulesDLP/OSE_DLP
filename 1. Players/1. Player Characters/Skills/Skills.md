# Skills
These house rules, taking inspiration from [Hyperbora 3rd Edition](https://www.hyperborea.tv/), make use of a d12 skill system.  Skills are broken up into 2 broad categories: common and class.  Common skills are those skills that all adventurers tend to develop over the course of wandering the wilderness, delving dungeons and fighting monsters.  Class skills are those skills that are only available to members of that class (normally, there are Knacks and Backgrounds that can modify this).

## Skill Chance
Each skill that a character has access to has a base success chance of 10+ on 1d12.  This reflects someone who's barely completed their apprenticeship.  While most common and class skills are available from 1st level onward, some class skills do not become available until higher levels.  These skills still start at a 10+.

## Advancing Skills
Starting at first level, and upon gaining each level, a character will gain a number of skill points with which to improve their skills.  Each point invested, lowers the target number for that skill by 1.  No more than 2 points may be allocated to any one skill at 1st level.  For every level after, only one point may be allocated to each skill per level.  No skill may be improved past a 2+ target number.

## Skill Check Procedure
The core mechanic for a skill check is simply rolling 1d12 and adding the appropriate [[5. Ability Scores|Ability Modifier]] (as determined by the GM).  If the total of the roll meets or exceeds the skill's target number, the skill check passes.

### When to Check
Skill checks are only needed if there is a meaningful chance of, and consequence to, failure.  Under normal circumstances with no real danger and plenty of time, it's best to assume the check simply succeeds.  However, the GM may be justified in calling for a check just to assess the quality of the result and how long it takes.

### Ones and Twelves
Rolling a 1 or a 12 is not any kind of critical or automatic success/failure.  The total of the roll still needs to be compared to the target number.

### Situational Modifiers
Situational modifiers are handled through the [[3d. Advantage and Disadvantage|Advantage/Disadvantage]] system.

### Retrying Skills
Normally, one cannot retry a failed skill roll.  Once it has failed, all further attempts will automatically fail.  This includes different characters attempting a skill check after another character has already failed (however, see [[#Assisting a Skill Checks]]).

There are a few exceptions:
- The character has [[4. Advancement|advanced a level]] since their last attempt.
- The character has improved their circumstances; either gaining an [[3d. Advantage and Disadvantage|Advantage]] they didn't have before, or removing a [[3d. Advantage and Disadvantage|Disadvantage]] they had previously.
- The GM determines that it is reasonable for a skill to be re-tried.  For example, someone falling out of a tree would, under normal circumstances, be fully warranted in getting up and trying to climb it again.  On the other hand, if someone searches a room a fails to find anything, all further searches will be fruitless unless the character does something to improve their odds.

### Group Skill Checks
In some cases an entire group is attempting the same thing at the same time (such as a party attempting to sneak past a group of guards).  In this case, everyone rolls at the same time and so long as 1/2 or more of the group succeeds, the skill check is considered a success.

### Assisting a Skill Checks
Characters may assist each other with skill checks, assuming they either have the same skill or they have a skill that could reasonably help under the circumstances.  For example, a mage attempting to brew a healing potion (using their Alchemy skill) could be assisted by a character with the Healing skill (as they presumably know about healing poultices and the like).  However, another character's Lockpicking skill has no relevance.

For each person assisting, the person performing the check gains a level of [[3d. Advantage and Disadvantage|Advantage]] on the check.  It is up to the GM to determine a reasonable limit on the number of helpers that can be added to a task (only so many cooks can work in the kitchen before they start tripping over each other).

### Opposed Rolls
In situations where rolls are opposed (such as an arm wrestling contest), both parties make their rolls and the results are compared as follows:
1. If one side succeeds and the other fails, the side that succeeded wins.
2. If neither side succeeds, the result is a stalemate (if reasonable).
3. If both sides succeed:
    1. If one side's check total is higher, that side wins.
    2. If both check totals are equal, the winner is whomever has the lower target number.
    3. If still tied, whomever has the most levels of Advantage (or fewest levels of Disadvantage) wins.
    4. If somehow still tied at this point, and a tie doesn't make sense, simply roll 1d6:  On a 1-3, side A wins; otherwise side B wins.
